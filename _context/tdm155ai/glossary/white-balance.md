# White balance

**The camera's setting for which color it treats as white, in kelvin, matched to the light's color temperature.**

## In plain terms

Tell the camera the light is 3200 K and it adds blue to cancel the orange; tell it 5600 K and it adds warmth. Auto white balance guesses from the scene and keeps re-guessing, so a shirt changes color as someone walks into frame. Lock it to the [color temperature](color-temperature.md) of your key light and the scene stays put.

Setting white balance "wrong" is a look: 3200 K under daylight goes cold and blue; 5600 K under tungsten goes warm and golden. Try both at station 7.

## Why it matters this term

Locked white balance is the difference between footage that cuts together and footage that shifts color every shot.

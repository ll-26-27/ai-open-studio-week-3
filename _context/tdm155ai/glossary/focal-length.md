# Focal length

**The distance from the lens to the sharp image when focused at infinity, in millimeters; it sets how wide or narrow the view is.**

## In plain terms

Hold a lens up to a wall and slide a sheet of ground glass behind it until a distant scene snaps sharp: that distance is the focal length. Short focal lengths (a 24 mm) see wide and push things apart; long ones (a 200 mm) see narrow and flatten distance. The 300 mm large-format lens at station 6 needs the glass about a foot behind it; the main iPhone lens is about 6 mm and sits millimeters from its sensor.

The "field of view" a focal length gives depends on [sensor size](sensor-size.md): 6 mm is a normal lens on a phone sensor and a fisheye on a full-frame camera. That is why phone lenses are quoted as "24 mm equivalent."

## Why it matters this term

Once you have found the focal length of a lens with your hands, the numbers on every camera stop being arbitrary.

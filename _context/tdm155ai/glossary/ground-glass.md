# Ground glass

**A sheet of glass frosted on one side so that a lens's image forms on it and can be seen from behind; the viewfinder of a large-format camera.**

## In plain terms

Light from a lens comes to a focus in the air, but you can't see an image in the air. A frosted surface catches it: the tiny scratches scatter the light so the image appears on the glass, upside down and reversed, and you can look at it, focus it, and measure it. Frosted acrylic and tracing paper work the same way; the texture of the grain differs.

Before film, this glass *was* the camera: the photographer composed on it under a dark cloth, then swapped in a plate. Photographing what's on the glass with a phone is a way of putting a modern sensor where the plate went.

## Why it matters this term

It turns the invisible geometry of a lens into something you can watch move.

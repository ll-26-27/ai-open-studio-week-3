# Hot spot

**The bright center of a rear-projected image, where the lens's light comes straight through the screen at your eye.**

## In plain terms

A frosted screen scatters light, but not evenly: light that came straight through toward you is stronger than light scattered off to the sides. So the image on the ground glass or acrylic looks brightest where your eye lines up with the lens and falls off toward the edges. Move your head and the hot spot moves with you. Rear-projection screens in cinemas have the same problem, and "screen gain" is the number that describes it.

When a phone photographs the glass it exposes for the hot spot, which pushes the rest of the image darker. Locking exposure in a manual app is the fix, and moving your phone a little off-axis is the other.

## Why it matters this term

It is the clearest example at the station of the recording stage adding something that wasn't in the world.

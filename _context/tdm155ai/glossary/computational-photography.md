# Computational photography

**What a phone does to an image before you ever see it: stacking frames, guessing depth, brightening faces, flattening contrast. The camera as an interpreter.**

## In plain terms

A phone's sensor is small and its lens is fixed, so the phone makes up the difference in software. It shoots several frames and merges them for HDR, denoises in the dark, segments the frame into sky and skin and treats each differently, guesses a depth map for portrait mode, and pushes faces toward a normal exposure. The result is often better than the raw sensor could do, and it is not what was in front of the lens.

Under dramatic lighting this works against you: the phone sees a dark side of a face and lifts it. A manual app with exposure locked is how you keep the lighting you built.

## Why it matters this term

It is why a phone shot of the studio-lit face looks flatter than the monitor, and why "just use your phone" is a choice with a cost.

# Workmanship of risk

**David Pye's term for making where the result is at stake the whole way through, as opposed to the workmanship of certainty, where the outcome is fixed in advance.**

## In plain terms

Pye, a woodworker and design theorist, drew the line in *The Nature and Art of Workmanship* (1968): carving freehand is workmanship of risk, because a slip changes the piece; printing from a plate is workmanship of certainty, because the plate decides. Neither is better; they make different things. The camera obscura station is all risk: the image depends on your hands on the glass. A phone snapshot is closer to certainty: the software decides.

This is the craft version of a wider idea (Tim Ingold's *Making*, Ruskin on the Gothic): form isn't a vision stamped onto passive material; it comes out of the friction between maker, tool, and material, and the marks that friction leaves are the interesting part.

## Why it matters this term

It gives you a name for the difference between what you did at the obscura station and what your phone does for you, and a reason to value the marks.

# Depth of field

**The band of distances that look sharp in a photo; shallow (thin) with big sensors and wide apertures, deep with small sensors and narrow ones.**

## In plain terms

Focus is only exact at one distance. In front of and behind it, things get gradually blurrier, and depth of field is how far you can go before you notice. Three things make it shallower: a wider [aperture](aperture.md), a longer [focal length](focal-length.md), and getting closer to the subject. A bigger [sensor](sensor-size.md) forces longer focal lengths for the same view, so big cameras have shallow focus almost by default.

On the ground glass, focus on a near object and watch the far one dissolve; close the iris and watch it come back. A phone, with a 6 mm lens, has such deep focus that almost everything is sharp, so its portrait mode has to blur the background in software from a depth guess, and gets hair and glasses wrong.

## Why it matters this term

It is the visual signature that separates "cinematic" from "phone," and today you see the physics that makes it.

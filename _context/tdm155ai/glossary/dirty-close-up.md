# Clean and dirty close-ups

**A clean close-up has only the subject in frame. A dirty one lets a sliver of the other person (a shoulder, hair) into the edge. Same scene, two feelings.**

## In plain terms

In a two-person scene the close-ups can be **clean** (the other person entirely out of frame) or **dirty** (a bit of their shoulder, ear, or hair in the near foreground, soft). Dirty keeps the other person present and the space real; it is the OTS tightened until only a trace of the near shoulder is left. Clean isolates the subject; it feels more intimate, or more alone, and it is what you cut to when the scene turns inward.

Frame the OTS, then slide the near shoulder out, then let a sliver back in. Capture all three.

## Why it matters this term

It is a tiny choice at the edge of the frame that changes what a conversation feels like.

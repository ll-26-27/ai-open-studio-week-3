# Inversion

**The image a lens forms is upside down and reversed left to right. Every camera flips it back for you; the ground glass doesn't.**

## In plain terms

Light travels in straight lines, so light from the top of the scene passes through the lens and lands at the bottom of the image, and left lands on the right. The picture on the ground glass is upside down and mirrored, and that is correct. View-camera photographers composed that way under a dark cloth; single-lens reflex cameras added a mirror and a prism to turn it right way up in the viewfinder; phones do it in software.

Photograph the glass with a phone and the phone records the inverted image faithfully. Turn the phone over, or rotate the photo, and it reads.

## Why it matters this term

It is the first surprise at the station, and the first thing every camera since has been built to hide.

# ISO

**How much the sensor's signal is amplified; low ISO is clean and needs light, high ISO works in the dark and adds noise.**

## In plain terms

Named after the film-speed standard it replaced. ISO 100 is the sensor's native, cleanest setting; each doubling (200, 400, 800…) is one stop brighter and a little grainier. Phones push ISO hard in dim rooms because their sensors are small, which is why low-light phone video is mushy: noise reduction smearing the grain.

The rule in a lit scene: set the lowest ISO and add light or open the aperture. Raise ISO only when you have run out of the other two.

## Why it matters this term

It is the setting auto mode reaches for first, and the one you should reach for last.

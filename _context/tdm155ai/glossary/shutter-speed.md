# Shutter speed

**How long each frame is exposed; in video, usually written as a shutter angle, with 180° (half the frame time) giving natural motion blur.**

## In plain terms

A still camera exposes for a fraction of a second: 1/50 s, 1/500 s. Video borrows a film-camera term: the **shutter angle**, the fraction of each frame's time the shutter is open. 180° means half, so at 24 fps each frame is exposed for 1/48 s. That gives the amount of motion blur eyes accept as normal. A small angle (45°) freezes motion and looks staccato (the beach landing in *Saving Private Ryan*); 360° smears it.

Faster shutter also means less light, so shutter, [ISO](iso.md), and [aperture](aperture.md) trade against each other. LED panels can flicker at some shutter speeds; 30 fps at 180° is safe with cheap ones.

## Why it matters this term

It is the setting that separates "video" from "film" motion, and phones set it wrong by default.

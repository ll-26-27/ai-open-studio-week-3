# Three-point lighting

**The standard portrait setup: a key light to shape the face, a fill to soften the shadow, a back light to separate the subject from the background.**

## In plain terms

Build it in order and look after each step. The **[key](key-light.md)** is the main source, off to one side and above eye level, and it makes the face three-dimensional. The **[fill](fill-light.md)** is a weaker source or a white card on the opposite side that decides how dark the shadow side gets. The **[back light](back-light.md)** sits behind the subject, aimed at the hair and shoulders, and draws an edge so they don't melt into the wall.

Three points is a starting grammar, not a rule. A window can be the key; a wall can be the fill; the back light can be a colored panel on the wall instead. Turn each one off in turn to see what it was doing.

## Why it matters this term

Every talking-head video you've ever seen is some version of this, and today you build it from two panels and a card.

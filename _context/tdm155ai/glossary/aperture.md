# Aperture

**The size of the opening in the lens, written as an f-number; wider (f/1.8) lets in more light and blurs more, narrower (f/8) lets in less and keeps more in focus.**

## In plain terms

Inside most lenses is an iris of overlapping blades. Open it and more light reaches the sensor; close it and less does. The f-number is focal length divided by the opening's diameter, so a smaller number means a bigger hole: f/2 is wide open, f/16 is a pinhole. Each "stop" (f/2 → f/2.8 → f/4 → f/5.6 → f/8) halves the light.

Aperture also sets [depth of field](depth-of-field.md). Wide open, only a thin slice is sharp and the background goes to [bokeh](bokeh.md); stopped down, front to back is sharp. Phone lenses have fixed apertures, usually wide, which is one reason they need "portrait mode" software to fake the blur, and can't fake the sharpness the other way.

## Why it matters this term

It is the second corner of the exposure triangle and the physical cause of blurry backgrounds.

# Key light

**The main light on a subject, placed to one side and above eye level so the face has shape.**

## In plain terms

Put the key at 10 or 2 o'clock (you at the center of the clock, facing 12), 2–3 ft away, 6–12 in above the eyes and tilted down. Look for two things: a soft shadow on the far side of the nose, and a catchlight, a small reflection of the light in both eyes. No nose shadow means the light is too close to the camera and the face is flat. Dark eye sockets mean it is too high.

Distance sets softness as much as brightness: moving the key closer makes it bigger relative to the face, so shadows soften, while it also gets brighter ([inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md)). Move it before you dim it.

## Why it matters this term

One light in the right place beats three in the wrong ones. Start here every time.

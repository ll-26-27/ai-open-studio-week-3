# Inverse-square law

**Light falls off with the square of distance: half the distance is four times the brightness.**

## In plain terms

Move a light from 4 ft to 2 ft and the subject gets four times as much light, not twice. Move it to 8 ft and they get a quarter. Two consequences for a small room: distance is a bigger dial than the dimmer, and a light close to the subject falls off so fast that the background behind them goes dark on its own, which is often what you want.

The same law explains why a lav six inches from the mouth beats a phone mic three feet away by so much.

## Why it matters this term

It is why "move it closer" is the first fix for both too dark and too flat.

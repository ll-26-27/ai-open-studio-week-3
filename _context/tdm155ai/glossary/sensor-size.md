# Sensor size

**The physical size of the chip that records the image; bigger sensors need bigger lenses, gather more light, and give shallower focus.**

## In plain terms

A phone's main sensor is about 10 mm across. A "full-frame" camera's is 36 mm, the size of a 35 mm film frame. A 4×5 large-format sheet is 100 mm × 125 mm. The lens has to make an image circle that covers the sensor, so bigger sensors mean bigger, longer lenses sitting further away, and that geometry is what gives them shallow [depth of field](depth-of-field.md).

Bigger also means each pixel collects more light, so less noise at the same [ISO](iso.md). The **crop factor** is the ratio between a sensor and full frame: a phone's is around 4, so its 6 mm lens acts like a 24 mm one would on a full-frame body.

## Why it matters this term

It is the single number that explains most of the difference between a phone and a "real" camera.

# Color temperature

**How warm or cool a light is, in kelvin: 3200 K is tungsten orange, 5600 K is daylight blue-white.**

## In plain terms

Every light source has a color. Candles and old bulbs are orange (around 2700–3200 K); midday sun and most LEDs set to "daylight" are blue-white (5600 K); shade and overcast go bluer still. Your eye corrects for it automatically; a camera does not, it does what [white balance](white-balance.md) tells it.

The rule is: don't mix. Two panels at different temperatures on one face give an orange side and a blue side that no setting can fix. Match the panels to each other, and to whatever room light is in the shot, or turn the room lights off.

## Why it matters this term

Mixed color temperature is the most common lighting mistake and the easiest to spot once you know the words for it.
